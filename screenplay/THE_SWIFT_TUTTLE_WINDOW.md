# THE SWIFT-TUTTLE WINDOW

**Format:** Feature Film Climactic Sequence  
**Setting:** Schnebly Hill Vista, Sedona, Arizona  
**Date:** August 12–13, 2024  
**Author:** Screenplay Draft  

---

### SCENE BREAKDOWN & ASTRONOMICAL CORRELATION

- **22:45 MST (Aug 12):** Ascent through Sedona monsoon aftermath; heavy cloud deck (88% coverage); lingering first-quarter moon washes out sky.
- **23:40 MST (Aug 12):** **The Turning Point:** Moonset occurs precisely at 11:40 PM as the moon dips behind Cathedral Rock. Ambient illumination plummets.
- **00:55 – 01:15 MST (Aug 13):** **The Cloud Break:** Historical weather records indicate an abrupt clearing as cloud cover drops from 76% to 15%. The Milky Way and Perseus radiant are revealed.
- **02:45 – 03:30 MST (Aug 13):** Perseid radiant ascends past 50° elevation; Earth's orbital "windshield effect" accelerates meteor frequency. Discovery of persistent ionization trains.
- **03:45 MST (Aug 13):** **The Climax:** Peak shower rates; detonation of a magnitude -8 Perseid bolide (fireball) casting moving shadows over Bell Rock and Cathedral Rock, leaving a 45-second glowing mesospheric train.

---

```
EXT. SCHNEBLY HILL ROAD - SEDONA, ARIZONA - NIGHT (AUGUST 12, 2024) - 22:45 MST

A battered 2008 Subaru Outback crawls up the jagged red-dirt switchbacks
of Schnebly Hill Road. Tires grind through loose basalt and red clay still
slick from the late afternoon monsoon storm.

Vapor rises from the wet earth. The scent of rain-soaked red sandstone
and saturated creosote bush—petrichor—is thick enough to taste.


INT. SUBARU OUTBACK - CONTINUOUS

DR. MAYA LIN (36) grips the steering wheel. She wears a faded fleece
jacket from Lowell Observatory and wire-rimmed glasses. In the passenger
seat sits a Pelican flight case holding a specialized intensified CCD
sky-patrol camera.

Beside her is ELI LIN (67). Flannel shirt over a thermal henley. His hands
are weathered, scarred by decades of grinding telescope mirrors and
soldering optical mounts. A hand-carved cedar walking stick rests against
his knees.

Above them through the windshield: gray, suffocating murk.

A heavy, bruised underbelly of monsoon stratocumulus blankets the entire
sky. Diffused behind the cloud ceiling in the southwest hangs a hazy,
washed-out smear of white light: the First Quarter Moon.

                          ELI
            (staring up)
      Eighty percent cloud deck if it's an
      inch. We should've stayed down in
      Cottonwood, Maya.

                          MAYA
      The convective boundary is collapsing,
      Dad. Look at the barometric trace on
      the dash. 1016 millibars and rising. The
      wind is backing from southwest to east-
      northeast.

                          ELI
      You can quote your NOAA telemetry all
      night, kiddo. Clouds don't read pressure
      gradients.

                          MAYA
      It's not just telemetry. It's the peak.
      August twelfth only happens once every
      365 days.

                          ELI
      Comet Swift-Tuttle has been orbiting the
      Sun every 133 years for the last twenty
      thousand years. It’s not going anywhere
      if we miss a night.

                          MAYA
            (quiet, tightening her grip)
      Swift-Tuttle isn't going anywhere. But
      you might.

A heavy silence fills the cab. Eli glances at his trembling left hand. He
curls his fingers into a fist, hiding it under his thigh.


EXT. SCHNEBLY HILL OVERLOOK - 23:25 MST

The Subaru idles at the lip of the scenic overlook.

Six thousand feet above sea level. Below them stretches the prehistoric bowl
of Sedona: the colossal shadows of Bell Rock, Courthouse Butte, and
Cathedral Rock rise like slumbering red titan monoliths from the desert
floor.

Eli stands leaning against his cedar stick, breath fogging faintly in the
crisp 67-degree mountain air.

Maya sets a heavy carbon-fiber tripod into the slickrock sandstone. She locks
her wide-field astrophotography rig into place, connecting a ruggedized field
laptop.

On screen, her full-sky radiometer reads:
`CLOUD COVER: 71% - LIGHTING: LUNAR SCATTER (53% ILLUM) - RAD: PERSEUS EL. 18°`

                          ELI
      How bad is the moon washing out your sensor?

                          MAYA
            (tapping keys)
      Terrible. The moon is fifty-three percent
      illuminated. It's sitting right at twenty-five
      degrees altitude in the southwest. Every photon
      of moonlight is bouncing off water droplets in
      these clouds. It's turning the whole sky into
      a bowl of milk.

                          ELI
            (looking at his vintage mechanical watch)
      Twenty-three thirty-five.

                          MAYA
      What?

                          ELI
      Your ephemeris. What time does the rock drop?

                          MAYA
      Eleven-forty.

                          ELI
      Five minutes.

Maya stops calibrating. She looks at her father. He’s staring out toward
Cathedral Rock.


EXT. SCHNEBLY HILL OVERLOOK - 23:39 MST

The wind dies. A profound stillness settles across the red rocks.

In the southwest, the First Quarter Moon reaches the jagged, castellated rim
of Cathedral Rock.

It touches the crimson sandstone parapet. For thirty seconds, the sandstone
glows an eerie, incandescent ruby-violet.

Then—like a theater dimmer pulling down to zero—the crescent slips behind the
rock.

The glare vanishes.

The silver fog turns deep, velvety slate-blue.

                          MAYA
            (whispering)
      Moonset. 23:40. Exactly on the mark.

                          ELI
      Now if only your clouds had the decency
      to follow it.

Above them, the sky remains an impenetrable gray ceiling.

Maya slumps down onto the tailgate of the Subaru, hugging her knees against
the chill.

                          MAYA
      We drove six hundred miles for an overcast
      ceiling.

                          ELI
      We drove six hundred miles to sit on a
      rock together.

                          MAYA
      I wanted you to see it with the new
      spectrometer, Dad. I calibrated the
      diffraction grating to isolate the 557-
      nanometer green line. The exact forbidden
      oxygen transition you taught me when I was nine.

                          ELI
            (smiles softly)
      On the roof in Flagstaff. With that beat-up
      60-millimeter refractor.

                          MAYA
      You made me memorize the numbers before you
      let me look through the eyepiece.

                          ELI
      "If you don't know what you're seeing,
      Maya, you're just looking at pretty sparks.
      If you know the physics, you're looking at
      the birth of the solar system."

                          MAYA
            (eyes glistening)
      One hundred and nine P slash Swift-Tuttle.
      Twenty-six kilometers across. Twice the size
      of the rock that killed the dinosaurs.

                          ELI
      And every August, Earth drives right into its
      slipstream.


EXT. SCHNEBLY HILL OVERLOOK - 00:55 MST (AUGUST 13, 2024)

00:55 on Maya’s laptop clock. Temperature: 71.4°F. Wind: 4.3 mph from the
northeast.

Eli is asleep in the passenger seat, his head resting against the cold window
glass.

Maya sits alone on the tailgate, holding a lukewarm thermos of coffee.

Suddenly—a subtle, ghostly shadow moves across her lap.

She blinks.

She looks up.

Overhead, a hairline fracture appears in the stratocumulus ceiling.

Then another.

A high-altitude northeasterly wind begins peeling the monsoon deck back like
an old canvas sail. The clouds don’t just drift—they evaporate into the dry
desert atmosphere.

In ten seconds flat, the ceiling tears open.

And through the rift pours the universe.

The Milky Way blazes down—unfiltered, incandescent, a glittering river of
silver dust stretching from Cygnus directly overhead down into the core in
Sagittarius.

Because Sedona is an International Dark Sky reserve, there is zero light
pollution. The red rocks below stand etched in starshine.

Maya scrambles to her feet. Her coffee mug rolls across the sandstone.

                          MAYA
      Dad.

She yanks open the passenger door.

                          MAYA (CONT'D)
      Dad! Wake up. Look.

Eli stirs, disoriented. He steps out of the car, leaning heavily on his
walking stick.

He tilts his head back.

His eyes widen. The starlight reflects in the lenses of his glasses.

                          ELI
      Sweet mother of God...

Through the pristine clearing, the northeastern sky rises sharp and cold.
Above Lee Mountain sits the constellation Perseus, framed by Cassiopeia's
celestial 'W'.

`CCD MONITOR: CLOUD COVER 15% - ATMOSPHERIC VISIBILITY: 10 KM - RADIANT EL: 38°`

                          MAYA
      The radiant's cleared forty degrees. We're on
      the leading hemisphere.

                          ELI
      Earth is turning into the windshield.

ZIP!

A razor-thin streak of white fire slices across the zenith. High velocity.
It vanishes in a tenth of a second.

                          ELI (CONT'D)
      There's one!

                          MAYA
      Too fast to track! Sixty kilometers a second!

ZIP! ZIP!

Two more streak across the Double Cluster in Perseus, parallel trajectories
diverging from the radiant point.

                          ELI
      Look at the trajectory. Trace it back.
      Right between Algol and Mirfak.

                          MAYA
            (checking the laptop sensor feed)
      Fifty-nine point four kilometers per second.
      That’s 132,000 miles per hour, Dad. Sand
      grains hitting the mesosphere ninety kilometers
      up.

                          ELI
      Not sand. Ice and stony chondrite. Left
      behind when Abraham Lincoln was in the White
      House.


EXT. SCHNEBLY HILL OVERLOOK - 02:45 MST

The temperature hovers at a calm 70.5°F. The sky is now 86% clear, transparent
as blown crystal.

Perseus climbs toward 50 degrees altitude. The meteors aren't just occasional
anymore; they arrive in bursts of three and four.

A bright Perseid streaks from Perseus toward Ursa Major, burning a vivid
EMERALD GREEN.

Behind it, hanging suspended in the black sky, is a glowing, pencil-thin green
line.

                          ELI
      Maya! Look at the wake! It's holding!

                          MAYA
      Persistent ionization train! Don't look away!

Maya swings the optical sensor onto the coordinate. The laptop screen zooms
in on the trail.

On screen, the glowing trail isn't static. It slowly bends. A kink forms in
the center, twisting the line into a delicate, luminous corkscrew.

                          ELI
      Look at that shear. The jet stream.

                          MAYA
      Mesospheric winds, sixty miles above our heads.
      Two hundred miles an hour, twisting ionized
      oxygen atoms like smoke.

                          ELI
            (whispering, captivated)
      Why the green, Maya? Tell me again.

                          MAYA
      Forbidden oxygen line. The neutral oxygen in the
      upper atmosphere gets pumped with kinetic energy
      from the meteoroid's shockwave. When the electrons
      finally decay back to the ground state... 557.7
      nanometers. Pure emerald.

Eli smiles, his face bathed in the soft glow of the laptop.

                          ELI
      You remember.

                          MAYA
      I remember every single word you told me.

Eli looks at her. His eyes are bright with unshed tears.

                          ELI
      I forget things now, Maya. Names of old
      coworkers. Where I left my keys. Some mornings
      I wake up and I don't know what year it is for
      ten seconds.

Maya freezes.

                          ELI (CONT'D)
      The doctors call it progressive cognitive
      impairment. I call it the slow dimming of the
      mirror.

                          MAYA
            (voice breaking)
      Why didn't you tell me?

                          ELI
      Because you're measuring the lifespan of
      galaxies. You're tracking objects that take a
      hundred and thirty-three years just to come back
      once. I didn't want you looking at my clock
      running down.

                          MAYA
      You're my father, Eli! The only reason I look at
      the stars is because you taught me to look up!

                          ELI
      And I wanted to give you one more night where
      we only looked up. Not back. Not forward.
      Just... here.

A tear spills over Eli’s weathered cheek. Maya steps toward him, wrapping her
arms tightly around his shoulders.

Eli rests his chin against her head.


EXT. SCHNEBLY HILL OVERLOOK - 03:45 MST

03:45 AM. The deepest hour of the night.

The Perseid radiant in Perseus has reached 58 degrees elevation.

Meteor rates have surged into a genuine celestial bombardment—one every thirty
to forty-five seconds. Silver darts, amber flashes, doublets skipping across
the upper atmosphere like stones across a black pond.

Suddenly—

A deafening silence seems to fall over the mesa.

A point of light erupts directly between the horns of Taurus in the east.

It doesn’t just streak. It DETONATES.

A BOLIDE.

A meteor the size of a fist punches through the mesosphere.

It expands into an incandescent, blinding magnesium-green fireball brighter
than the full moon.

For two full seconds, the entire desert turns DAYLIGHT GREEN.

The jagged crimson spires of Cathedral Rock, Bell Rock, and Courthouse Butte
cast razor-sharp, moving black shadows across the red sandstone amphitheater.

Maya and Eli's long shadows whip across the bedrock behind them.

The fireball surges in brightness—flashing brilliant white-cyan—then breaks
into three luminous fragments before vanishing in a silent terminal puff.

Darkness slams back over the desert like a shutter.

Both of them stand frozen in absolute shock.

                          ELI
            (gasping)
      Did you... did you see that?

                          MAYA
            (trembling)
      Magnitude minus-eight bolide. The sensor...
      look at the sensor!

On the field laptop, the camera display shows the afterglow.

A massive, ribbon-like train of glowing ionized gas hangs directly where the
bolide detonated. It glows vivid turquoise and amber, slowly billowing in the
mesospheric wind like a celestial aurora.

Ten seconds pass.

Twenty seconds.

Thirty seconds.

The glowing ghost of the comet still hangs suspended against the background
stars of the Pleiades.

Eli lifts his hands, palms up to the sky, as if warming them by a fire that
burned out sixty miles above the earth.

                          ELI
      Two thousand years in the dark... just to burn
      like that.

                          MAYA
            (taking his hand)
      Not burned out, Dad. Transformed.

Eli grips her hand tightly. His fingers don't shake anymore.

High above the silent red towers of Sedona, the glowing green train slowly
diffuses into the cosmos, joining the endless river of starlight.

In the east, over Lee Mountain, the first imperceptible hint of astronomical
twilight begins to touch the rim of the world.

                                                      FADE OUT.
```
